Hyderabad Engineer Builds Systems That Refuse To Fall Over

Gopal Kataria, an Electronics & Communication student at IIIT Hyderabad, has spent three years shipping ticketing platforms, file-transfer daemons and Hindi speech models. He would like to do more of it.

By Our Staff Correspondent · Hyderabad

HYDERABAD — Gopal Kataria does not use a desktop environment. His GitHub bio calls them bloat and recommends vim, lynx and tmux instead. That says a lot about how he builds software: keep it lean, and understand every layer.

Kataria is a B.Tech + MS (by Research) student in Electronics and Communication Engineering at IIIT Hyderabad, class of 2028. His work runs the length of the stack: TLS and epoll in C, Go and Postgres under festival-day traffic, and lately, PyTorch models that speak Hinglish.

This summer, as an AI research intern at ixigo, he built a Hindi/Hinglish speech corpus from real call-centre recordings and LoRA fine-tuned an open-source text-to-speech model. Served with vLLM, it starts streaming audio in about 140 ms, on par with the commercial provider in production. Business, B1

On campus he is better known for keeping things up. The Felicity 2026 registration platform, built by the team he led, stayed up through last-minute requirement changes. Zero downtime. Technology, D1

Between deployments he studies change-point detection for wireless signals at SPCRC, ran the Open Source Developers Group for a year, and once made a 7-billion-parameter language model forget Harry Potter. Science, C1

Asked what comes next, Kataria pointed to harder problems in distributed systems and ML infrastructure. Then he went to get biriyani.

AI Research Intern · ixigo

Travel App’s Voice Bot Learns To Pronounce Thiruvananthapuram

An intern’s LoRA-tuned Hindi/Hinglish speech model streams its first audio in about 140 ms, on par with the commercial provider in production.

May – July 2026

Ask a text-to-speech model to read out a PNR number, a fare in rupees or a station in Kerala, and it will usually stumble somewhere. Kataria’s fix started with the data.

He built a Hindi/Hinglish corpus from real call-centre recordings, processing the audio with Librosa and Pandas. He then cut targeted datasets for the places TTS systems usually go wrong: numerals, travel terms, code-mixed Hinglish and Indian place names.

He LoRA fine-tuned an open-source Hindi/Hinglish TTS model with PEFT and Hugging Face Transformers, and served it with vLLM on Modal. Streaming first-byte latency came in at around 140 ms, comparable to the commercial provider in production.

Undergraduate Researcher · SPCRC, IIIT-H

Something Changed In The Signal. He Wants To Know Exactly When.

A new change-point detection framework for transmitted wireless signals, with theoretical guarantees to match.

May 2025 – present · Manuscript in preparation

When a transmitted signal changes, whether a new emitter appears, a channel degrades or someone starts jamming, the receiver should notice quickly without raising false alarms. That trade-off is the subject of sequential change-point detection, and of Kataria’s research at the Signal Processing and Communications Research Centre.

He has developed a new detection framework for transmitted wireless signals, aimed at surveillance and defence communications. He derived theoretical performance guarantees for it and backed them with numerical and simulation-based analysis. A manuscript is in preparation.

Language Model Made To Forget Harry Potter; Muggles Unaffected

Ablating sparse-autoencoder features cut Harry Potter recall by 77% for a 0.1% rise in perplexity.

Using a 16,384-feature sparse-autoencoder dictionary on LLaMA-7B’s residual stream, Kataria used TransformerLens to find the features that fire on Harry Potter knowledge, then ablated them. Recall fell 77%, perplexity rose just 0.1%, and the edit was four times more tightly localised.

No Cloud Required: Daemon Moves Files Across The LAN, Trusts No One

LANdrop is a peer-to-peer file-transfer daemon for Linux, written in C. Peers find each other over UDP, an epoll-driven event loop handles the connections, and files move in OpenSSL/TLS-secured chunks verified with SHA-256. New peers are pinned on first use and remembered in a persistent trust store.

Op-Ed

Your Desktop Environment Is Bloat

Gopal Kataria builds systems at IIIT Hyderabad. His dotfiles are public; judge accordingly.

I have nothing against desktop environments. I just don’t need one, and I suspect you don’t either.

Over the years I’ve run i3 with polybar, then sway, then Hyprland, with tint2 holding things together in between. These days it all lives in a dotfiles repo and a NixOS config, so any machine I sit at can be mine in minutes, and I know why every process on it is running.

Lean tools make you curious. If your browser is lynx, you learn what a web page actually is. If your editor is vim, you stop reaching for the mouse. If your session lives in tmux, a dropped SSH connection is a minor inconvenience.

The same instinct shows up in my work: understand every layer, then remove the ones you don’t need. You don’t have to go all the way. But try a week with a tiling window manager. You might not come back.
